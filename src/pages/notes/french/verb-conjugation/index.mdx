# French verb conjugation study

{/* cSpell:disable */}

Data source: [Josh-Cena/french-inflections](https://github.com/Josh-Cena/french-inflections)

The following words, while correct, are removed from the dataset because they are obsolete or have very rare patterns:

- copier-coller: Complicated to analyze because both parts conjugate
- ruisseller: now spelled as ruisseler, and listed with conjugations like -eler verbs instead of like other -eller verbs
- estre: now spelled as être, and so archaic that the endings are Middle French (e.g., "ois" instead of "ais" for imperfect)
- brusler: now spelled as brûler, and listed with Middle French conjugation
- esloigner: now spelled as éloigner, and listed with Middle French conjugation
- esposer: now spelled as exposer, and listed with Middle French conjugation
- resver: now spelled as rêver. Slightly hard to predict because present 1sg is "resve" and not "rèsve"
- oevrer: now spelled as œuvrer. Removed just so I don't have to say why it's not "oèvre".

The data encodes alternative spellings in inflections as `;`. The infinitive may also have spelling variations (mostly stemming from the [1990 spelling reform](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Appendix:French_spelling_reforms_of_1990)) but does not pertain to conjugation patterns, so they are treated as independent words. There are only three rows with identical infinitive spellings:

- départir: Selon l'Académie, départir et se départir se conjuguent comme partir (en se départant ; je me dépars, il se départ, etc.), mais l'usage leur fait généralement suivre la conjugaison de répartir (en se départissant ; je me départis, il se départit, etc.). [source](https://www.larousse.fr/conjugaison/francais/d%C3%A9partir/2847)
- ressortir: irregular conjugation when meaning "to go out again", regular conjugation when meaning "to come under the jurispendence of". [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ressortir#French)
- faillir: irregular conjugation when meaning "to almost", regular conjugation when meaning "to go bankrupt". [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/faillir#French)

My final dataset contains 5344 verbs. It has 4694 -er verbs, 360 -ir verbs, 37 -oir verbs, 251 -re verbs, and 2 -ïr verbs: haïr, ouïr.

### Group 1

Group 1 -er conjugation is as follows:

| Inf | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl  | 2pl | 3pl  | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---- | --- | ---- | ----- |
| -er | -e  | -es | -e  | -ons | -ez | -ent | 4073  |

No alternations are made if and only if the root (infinitive minus -er) are all true:

- The root does not end with -g or -c, unless it ends with -gg.
- The last syllable's vowel is not é. (Note: a syllable is counted as VC(C), where CC might be "gu" or "qu" but must otherwise be consonants.)
- The last syllable's vowel is not e, unless the e is followed by w, x, or y, or the word is "clamecer". Interestingly, "clamecer" conjugates as "clamece" [klams] without "è", but "dépecer" (therefore "redépecer") conjugates as "dépèce" [de.pɛs]. ([Wikitionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/clamecer#French))
- The root does not end with oy, uy, or ay.

The rest undergo phonological changes.

First, the following "g" and "c" softening rules are purely orthographic so happens across all tenses:

- When the final consonant is g [ʒ], an extra e is added before the suffix if the suffix is open (starts with a, o, â). (With the exception of "tagger" which is already a hard g; it is fully regular.)
- When the final consonant is c, it is changed to ç before the suffix if the suffix is open.

| Inf | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl | 2pl | 3pl | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -**g**er | -**g**e | -**g**es | -**g**e | -**ge**ons | -**g**ez | -**g**ent | 147 |
| -**c**er | -**c**e | -**c**es | -**c**e | -**ç**ons | -**c**ez | -**c**ent | 97 |

For all other words, alternations are never made to the 1pl and 2pl forms, for which the suffix is not silent. For 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, and 3pl (-e, -es, -ent), the ending is silent and the root would be rewritten as follows:

- When the last syllable's vowel is é, the é is changed to è. -éger and -écer also simultaneously use the above two rules.
- When the last syllable's vowel is e, unless the e is followed by t, l, w, x, or y (in other words: -ev, -evr, -es, -ec, -em, -en), the e is changed to è. -eger and -ecer also simultaneously use the above two rules. "clamecer" is the only exception.
- When the last syllable's vowel is e, and the e is followed by t or l, there are three options: double the t or l (for -jeter and -appeler series), change the e to è, or either (words that previously could only double). There are only a handful of words that can do the second (never double):
  - acheter, cacheter, racheter, tacheter, crocheter, haleter, fureter, corseter, craqueter, débequeter, déchiqueter, duveter
  - celer, receler, déceler, déficeler, ensorceler, remodeler, -geler, grommeler, peler, ciseler, ruisseler, démanteler, -arteler, -niveler
- When the word would end on -oy or -uy, the y is changed to i.
- When the word would end on -ay, the y is changed to i _optionally_.

### Group 3

There are two main kinds of group 3 conjugations, which differ in their 3sg suffix. I will refer to them as subgroup 1 and subgroup 2, respectively. The first kind has no suffix for 3sg; the second kind has an extra "t" in 3sg. This group is very rough; in reality, very few words conjugate with these suffixes as-is. Most have some form of root mofification.

Start with -re.

-cre (convaincre, vaincre) words are subgroup 1, where the plurals replace "c" with "qu".

| Inf | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl | 2pl | 3pl | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -**c**re | -**c**s | -**c**s | -**c** | -**qu**ons | -**qu**ez | -**qu**ent | 2 |

Most -dre verbs are subgroup 1, except -indre and -soudre which are subgroup 2.

- -prendre: the plurals lose the "d", and softens "pren-" to "prenn-" for 3pl.
- -indre: all forms lose the "d", and plurals velarize "n" to "gn".
- -coudre and -moudre: the plurals replace "d" with "s" and "l". From [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/coudre#French):

  > _coudre_: The original Old French infinitive was \*_co(u)sre_, where _-d-_ was inserted as a gliding sound. The preconsonantal _-s-_ then became silent, in this case dropping from the spelling relatively early on. The _d_-spellings in the present tense are merely analogical (for expected _je cous_, _il coût_).
  >
  > _moudre_: The original form of the infinitive was \*_molre_, where _-d-_ was inserted as a gliding sound. [...]

- -soudre: the singulars remove the "d", while the plurals replace "ud" with "lv". From [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/soudre#French):

  > The forms with _-lv-_ are of learned origin; spoken Middle French had developed analogical forms with _-oud-_, which are attested until the 18th century (_absoudons_, _absoudait_ etc.).

- All other -dre (-(ép)andre, -(des)cendre, -fendre, (compre)hendre, -pendre, rendre, -tendre, -vendre, -ondre, -rdre): no root modification. I will refer to these 43 words as "regular -dre", which is useful later.

All -ire words are subgroup 2 (faire, dire, redire are irregular). Generally, the plurals get an additional -s. If yet another -s is added to the plurals, they would become group 2—this is the case for maudire and bruire.

- -(p)laire (complaire, déplaire, plaire): the 3sg form may optionally have a circumflex.
- -raire (braire, -traire), -(c)roire (croire, recroire): the plurals use -ray-/-roy- instead of -rais-/-rois- (where "y" becomes "i" in 3pl). From [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/traire#French):

  > The root -trais- was used instead of -tray- in the 18th century, and remains in Swiss and Savoy dialects.

- (ma)udire, bruire: group 2.
- -boire: 1pl and 2pl use buv- instead of boi-. From [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/boire#French):

  > The imperfect, 1st and 2nd plural present (including subjunctive), plural imperative and present participle stems are from Old French unstressed stem _bev-_ which [e]volves to _beuv-_, then _buv-_.

- rire, (so)urire: no root modification.
- frire: defective, but otherwise works like rire/sourire.
- -crire: plurals have an additional -v instead of -s.
- All other -ire (taire, (circon)cire, (pr)ædire, (con)tredire, -édire, (inte)rdire, -fire, -lire, -cuire, -duire, -luire, nuire, -truire): just add -s for plurals.

- -mouvoir (mouvoir, émouvoir, promouvoir): the singulars replace "ouv" with "eu", and the 3pl replaces "ouv" with "euv".

### Filling in defective verbs

Before continuing: there are certain tenses (notably, imperfect, subjunctive, imperative) that rely on certain present conjugations. If a verb is defective and lacks those conjugations, we have to figure out the missing forms separately. For this purpose, I will apply the rules above to the defective verbs. There are 20 words with missing present conjugations:

- -er: bruiner, brumer, crachiner, dracher, gibouler, grêler, neigeoter, pleuviner, pleuvioter, résulter, venter
  - By analogy with regular -er verbs
- -ger: neiger, urger
  - By analogy with regular -ger verbs
- -rire: frire
  - By analogy with rire/sourire
- -choir: échoir
  - By analogy with choir
- -vre: ensuivre
  - By analogy with suivre
- -llir: saillir
  - By analogy with assaillir
- -voir: pleuvoir, repleuvoir
  - Guessed endings: s/s/t/vons/vez/vent
- falloir
  - By analogy with faillir: faux/faux/faut/fallons/fallez/fallent

## Imperfect

The imperfect is formed based on the present 1pl form.

| 1pl (present) | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl | 2pl | 3pl | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -ons | -ais | -ais | -ait | -ions | -iez | -aient | 5088 |
| -**ge**ons | -**ge**ais | -**ge**ais | -**ge**ait | -**g**ions | -**g**iez | -**ge**aient | 155 |
| -**ç**ons | -**ç**ais | -**ç**ais | -**ç**ait | -**c**ions | -**c**iez | -**ç**aient | 100 |

Note that if the present 1pl form has multiple alternatives, the imperfect considers all of them. For example, "asseoir" has both "asseyons" and "assoyons" as 1pl present, so it has both "asseyais" and "assoyais" as imperfect forms. There are only 3 such verbs: asseoir, rasseoir, ouïr.

There's only one irregular imperfect. être has present 1pl "sommes", but imperfect formed with the suppletive "ét-".

| Inf  | Imperfect root |
| ---- | -------------- |
| être | ét-            |

## Future & conditional

The future is formed by directly appending to the infinitive. If the infinitive ends in "e", we remove it before appending.

| Inf | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl  | 2pl | 3pl  | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---- | --- | ---- | ----- |
| -   | -ai | -as | -a  | -ons | -ez | -ont | 4632  |
| -e  | -ai | -as | -a  | -ons | -ez | -ont | 242   |

The rest undergo a few root modifications. The first few are almost exactly the same as present [group 1](#group-1).

- If the infinitive ends in -éXer, the "é" is optionally changed to "è" (mandatory for present).
- If the infinitive ends in -eXer where X is anything other than t, l, w, x, y (but not clamecer), the "e" is changed to "è".
- If the infinitive ends in -eXer where X is t, l, there are three options: double the t or l, change the e to è, or either. Which one to use is exactly the same as before.
- If the infinitive ends in -oyer (except -envoyer), -uyer, the "y" is changed to "i".
- If the infinitive ends in -ayer, the "y" is optionally changed to "i".

| -ayer | -ayer;-aier  | 27    |

The remaining are irregular. First, almost all -oir verbs are irregular, except the following 5: dépourvoir, pourvoir, prévoir, surseoir, échoir. Then, there are 14 that all conjugate by removing -oir and adding -r: -cevoir (apercevoir, concevoir, décevoir, entrapercevoir, percevoir, recevoir, repercevoir), -devoir (devoir, redevoir), -mouvoir (mouvoir, promouvoir, émouvoir), -(pl)euvoir (pleuvoir, repleuvoir). ## Imperative

Generally, the imperative forms are just the present forms. For 2sg, if the present 2sg form ends in -es, the -s is dropped. This includes -er and -ir infinitives. Specially, "vas" (aller) also becomes "va".

| 2sg (present) | 2sg | Count |
| ------------- | --- | ----- |
| -es           | -e  | 4710  |
| -             | -   | 630   |
| vas           | va  | 1     |

There are 3 exceptions:

| Inf    | 2sg   | 1pl     | 2pl    |
| ------ | ----- | ------- | ------ |
| être   | sois  | soyons  | soyez  |
| avoir  | aie   | ayons   | ayez   |
| savoir | sache | sachons | sachez |

## Present participle

The present participle is formed based on the present 1pl form.

| 1pl (present) | PP   | Count |
| ------------- | ---- | ----- |
| -ons          | -ant | 5339  |

| avoir  | ayant        |
